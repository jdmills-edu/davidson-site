History of Davidson

Origins

Davidson traces its roots to 1837, when a group of Presbyterians established Davidson College on land donated from the estate of Brigadier General William Lee Davidson. General Davidson was a hero of the American Revolution who was killed at the Battle of Cowan's Ford in 1781 while defending against British forces crossing the Catawba River.

Town and Gown

For its first several decades, the community was known simply as "Davidson College." In 1891, the town officially shortened its name to "Davidson," reflecting the growing identity of the community beyond the college campus. By 1910, the town had roughly 500 residents, climbing to about 1,500 by the 1920s.

Industry and Growth

The late 19th century brought industry to Davidson. The Linden Cotton Factory opened in 1890 on Depot Street, becoming the town's first textile plant. In 1908, the Delburg Cotton Mills was constructed nearby. These mills shaped the town's economy and demographics for decades.

Modern Davidson

Davidson experienced dramatic growth in the late 20th and early 21st centuries as the Charlotte metropolitan area expanded northward. The population grew from around 4,000 in 1990 to over 15,000 by 2020. Despite this growth, Davidson has worked to preserve its small-town character through careful planning, a walkable downtown, and strong community institutions.
